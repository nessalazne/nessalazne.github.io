Most creators watch a viral video three or four times, feel like they understand it, and then make something that doesn't work nearly as well. The gap is that watching a video and breaking it down are two different skills, and AI is much better at the second one than most people expect.

| Breakdown part | What it tells you | What you do with it |
|---|---|---|

## How Do You Spot Trends Across Multiple Videos?

You spot a real trend by running the same breakdown on three to five videos in your niche and comparing the results side by side, not by watching one video and guessing. One video tells you what one creator did. Five videos in the same category, broken down the same way, tell you what the format actually rewards.

This is the part that's easy to skip because it's slower than analyzing one video, but it's also the part that turns a hunch into something you can act on with confidence. Growth OS runs several video breakdowns together and surfaces the overlap automatically, with a visual report showing what to make next. If you're doing it manually in Claude, you're doing the same comparison, just one conversation at a time.

## How Do You Remake a High-Performing Video Without Copying It?

You remake a high-performing video by keeping its structure and replacing its content, which is different from copying it and different from being "inspired by" it in a way that doesn't actually transfer anything useful. The hook pattern, the beat order, and the pacing are reusable. The specific topic, footage, and wording are not.

Once you have the breakdown from the section above, the remake step is mechanical:

1. Take the hook pattern and write three versions of it using your own topic
2. Map your own story or point onto the same beat order as the original
3. Match the pacing (when text appears, when the cut happens) rather than the exact visuals

### Do I need Growth OS to reverse-engineer a video with AI?

### How many videos do I need to compare before I trust a trend?

Three to five videos in the same niche, broken down with the same categories each time. One video shows you a choice one creator made. Several videos, compared side by side, show you what the format is actually rewarding right now.

### What's the difference between remaking a video and copying it?

Remaking a video means reusing its structure (the hook pattern, the order of ideas, the pacing) with your own topic and words. Copying means reusing the specific wording or footage. Platforms and audiences both respond differently to the two, and only the first one holds up over time.

### Can Claude actually analyze a video directly?

Claude can't watch a video file, so you give it the transcript and a written description of the visual layout (or use a tool like Niche Pulse that extracts both automatically). Claude is accurate at breaking down text it's actually given and unreliable if you ask it to guess at visuals it hasn't seen.
